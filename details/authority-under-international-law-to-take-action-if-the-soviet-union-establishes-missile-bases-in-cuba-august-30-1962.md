## Overview

Dated August 30, 1962, this Office of Legal Counsel memo was prepared in response to U-2 spy plane photographs showing surface-to-air missile sites under construction in Cuba. It provided legal analysis for the Kennedy administration regarding potential U.S. actions.

## Key Points

- The memo concluded that international law would permit the United States to use relatively extreme measures, including various forms and degrees of force, in response to Soviet missile bases in Cuba.
- It acknowledged an obligation to observe a rule of proportionality.
- The memo discussed the Monroe Doctrine as an explicit qualification on general legal concepts in the Western Hemisphere, aimed at preventing European or Asiatic power bloc influence.
- It examined the tension between bloc security concepts and traditional balance-of-power doctrines.

## Historical Context

Written six weeks before the Cuban Missile Crisis, the memo reflects the Kennedy administration's early legal deliberations upon discovering Soviet missile installations in Cuba.

## Relevance

The memo is notable for its early consideration of proportionality and bloc security concepts that informed the eventual resolution of the Cuban Missile Crisis.
