## Overview

George Kennan's Long Telegram was a 5,300-word diplomatic cable sent from the U.S. Embassy in Moscow to the State Department on February 22, 1946.

## Content

In the telegram, Kennan analyzed the Soviet leadership's worldview, arguing that it was driven by a traditional Russian sense of insecurity and a Marxist-Leninist ideology that viewed international relations as inherently conflictual. He concluded that the Soviet Union was impervious to logic of reason but highly sensitive to logic of force, and that it could be contained through firm and vigilant resistance.

## Historical Context

The telegram was sent in response to U.S. inquiries about Soviet reluctance to cooperate on postwar plans. It provided the intellectual foundation for the U.S. policy of containment during the Cold War.

## Significance

The Long Telegram influenced key policymakers, including Secretary of State James F. Byrnes, and contributed to the development of the Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan. It is considered a seminal document in Cold War history.
