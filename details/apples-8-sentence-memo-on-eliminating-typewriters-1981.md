## Overview

Apple's 8-Sentence Memo on Eliminating Typewriters was issued in October 1981 by then-CEO Michael Scott. The memo directed all employees to cease purchasing or leasing typewriters and to adopt word processing technology immediately, with a goal of eliminating typewriters from Apple by January 1, 1981.

## Memo Content

> EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY!! NO MORE TYPEWRITERS ARE TO BE PURCHASED, LEASED, etc., etc.
> 
> Apple is an innovative company. We must believe and lead in all areas. If word processing is so neat, then let’s all use it!
> 
> Goal: by 1-1-81, NO typewriters at Apple… We believe the typewriter is obsolete. Let’s prove it inside before we try and convince our customers.

## Context and Impact

At the time, typewriters were still prevalent in offices, but Apple sought to lead by example in adopting emerging word processing technology. The memo exemplified Apple's philosophy of internal innovation adoption before customer-facing promotion. This decision contributed to Apple's early embrace of personal computing tools and reinforced its culture of technological leadership.

## Relevance Today

The memo is frequently cited in business leadership and innovation discussions as an example of "eating your own dog food" — using your own products internally to validate their effectiveness before market release.
