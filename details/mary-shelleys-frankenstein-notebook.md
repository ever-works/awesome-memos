# Mary Shelley's Frankenstein Notebook

## Overview
This collection of notebooks contains the original handwritten draft of Mary Shelley's novel *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus*, one of the most influential works of Gothic literature.

## Creation Process
The story emerged during the summer of 1816, known as the 'Year Without a Summer,' when Mary Shelley, Percy Bysshe Shelley, Lord Byron, and John Polidori gathered near Lake Geneva. They exchanged ghost stories, and Shelley conceived the idea for Frankenstein after a waking dream. She wrote the draft in her notebooks over the following months.

## Historical Significance
*Frankenstein* is considered the first true science fiction novel, exploring themes of creation, responsibility, and the dangers of unchecked scientific ambition. The notebooks reveal her creative process, revisions, and the literary influences that shaped the work.

## Current Location
The notebooks are preserved and digitized in the Shelley-Godwin Archive, providing scholars and the public with high-quality access to the original manuscripts.
