# Shrimp and Weenies Memo

## Overview

The 'Shrimp and Weenies' memo is a renowned internal company memo written by Mike Murray at Microsoft in the early 1990s. It was circulated company-wide to address rising travel expenses without resorting to strict, bureaucratic guidelines.

## Meaning

The memo's title comes from Murray's humorous analogy: employees should travel as if they were paying for their own shrimp and weenies, encouraging cost-consciousness while treating company resources as their own. It conveyed a mindset of frugality and ownership rather than imposing rigid rules.

## Creation Process

Mike Murray, a Microsoft manager at the time, crafted the memo after observing that traditional travel policy memos were ineffective. He chose a conversational, relatable tone, using vivid metaphors and a personal voice to engage employees. The memo avoided jargon and instead focused on shared values and practical advice.

## Context

In the early 1990s, Microsoft faced rapidly increasing travel costs as the company expanded. Instead of issuing a standard policy memo filled with do's and don'ts, Murray sought to inspire behavioral change through storytelling and empathy, aligning with the company's culture of innovation and employee empowerment.

## Impact and Legacy

The memo became legendary within Microsoft and beyond as an exemplar of effective internal communication. It demonstrated that memos could be both informative and engaging, influencing how companies approach policy announcements. Decades later, it continues to be cited in business writing and management training as a model of memorable, human-centered messaging.

## Key Takeaways

- Use metaphors and storytelling to make policies relatable.
- Speak as a real person, not a corporate entity.
- Focus on underlying values rather than exhaustive rules.
- Humor and personality can increase engagement and retention.
