## Overview

The Truman Doctrine was a policy set forth by U.S. President Harry S. Truman on March 12, 1947, promising economic and military aid to Greece and Turkey to prevent them from falling into the Soviet sphere.

## Significance

- Marked the beginning of the Cold War containment strategy.
- Established the principle of U.S. support for free peoples resisting subjugation.
- Led to substantial American aid programs in Europe and beyond.

## Context

Issued amid the Greek Civil War and Soviet pressure on Turkey, the doctrine responded to fears of communist expansion in the Eastern Mediterranean.
