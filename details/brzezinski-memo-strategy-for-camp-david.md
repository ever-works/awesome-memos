# Brzezinski Memo: Strategy for Camp David

## Overview

Authored by Zbigniew Brzezinski on August 31, 1978, this five-page memo was directed to President Jimmy Carter ahead of the Camp David negotiations between Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin. The document outlines a clear strategy for controlling the negotiation proceedings and achieving a peaceful agreement.

## Key Contents

- **Opening Objective**: Carter must 'control the proceedings from the outset' and pursue a deliberate strategy to shift entrenched positions.
- **Analysis of Leaders**: Brzezinski notes that Sadat cannot afford failure, while Begin may believe a failure would hurt Carter and Sadat but not himself, suggesting pressure points.
- **Risk Warnings**: Advises avoiding diversion by Begin’s legalism or Sadat’s imprecision; urges staying focused on core issues and referring sudden proposals to foreign ministers.
- **Separate Negotiations**: Stresses the importance of negotiating separately with each leader to uncover fallback positions.
- **Acceptable Minimum**: Concludes with four points outlining the minimum outcomes the U.S. must secure, including Israel’s phased withdrawal from occupied territories.

## Historical Context

The memo was among approximately 1,400 pages of CIA documents released concerning the Camp David Accords. It exemplifies the meticulous preparation credited with the success of the negotiations—the only successful Middle East peace brokered by a U.S. president to that point.

## Significance

Beyond its immediate diplomatic impact, the memo is celebrated for its concise, precise, and analytical writing style. It demonstrates how thorough preparation and clear strategic communication can influence high‑stakes negotiations, offering lessons for leadership, management, and conflict resolution.
